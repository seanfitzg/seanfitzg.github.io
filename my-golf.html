Personal Yardage Book · Swing Notes

Pre-Round Swing Card

Hover the club → slow straight-back one-piece start (club, arms & chest away together) → full free turn → hands above the trail shoulder → (nothing) → full finish: hips & chest facing the target, balanced on the front side.

1

Setup

  • Stand closer — arms hang relaxed, not reaching. Butt of the club: 4 fingers for wedges–7i, add the thumb for long irons & hybrids, full hand-span for driver.
  • Hover the club — lift the head off the ground before starting back (pro's tip). Forces soft hands automatically, kills the jerky start, keeps the wrists alive. A setup condition beats a thought you have to remember.
  • Chin up a touch — a small lift at address clears room for the shoulders to turn under it. Not a head raise, just unblocking the neck. All clubs.
  • Weight in the middle of your feet — arches, not the balls. Feeling like you're "sitting back" is just the correction from a toe-heavy habit. Toes should lift freely inside your shoes.
  • Too far away + weight on the toes = reaching, standing up through impact → thin, low, sliced. Check this first, before any swing fix.
2

Backswing

  • Slow, one-piece takeaway — arms, chest, hips start away together.
  • Full turn, hips free — back to the target, head swivels, ball in the left eye at the top.
  • Straight back & low — a feel, first 12" only. The chest turn makes the real path arc gently inside; forcing it straight further would sway or shove the club outside. Counters my old inside whip. Check at shaft parallel: clubhead in line with or just outside the hands, shaft parallel to the target line.
  • Hands stay in front of the chest — the corridor. Two rails from your shoulders that rotate with you; the club only moves because the chest turned.
  • Soft hands at the top — grip pressure 4–5/10. Tension blocks the release, rushes transition, and lets the forearms twist the club flat. You can't over-do "soft." Fixed a poor session on its own.
  • Hands finish above the trail shoulder — a destination, not a lift. Late in the backswing the arms go up, never around. Your unattended default is flat & laid-off (club wrapped behind you) → club stuck, blocks/hooks, fats. Aim at the position; don't push the arms into it.
3

Downswing

  • Hips on autopilot — never a conscious trigger.
  • No downswing thought — your best strikes come from an empty mind in the middle.
  • One thought: the full finish — hips & chest facing the target, stacked on the front side. A destination, not a trigger — you can't get there with a broken sequence.
  • Pulls + slices or thins = chest fired first. High + short = hips fired hard. Cure: slow start, picture the finish. Bad-day rescue: "hips start, full finish" — nothing else.
  • Don't swing hard. Effort grabs from the top, shortens the backswing, and costs posture → tops and thins. Speed comes from turn & sequence — put the effort in the finish, never the top.
4

Driver

  • Ball off lead heel, tee high, spine tilted away.
  • Head behind the ball at impact — swing up at it.
5

Chipping

  • Feet & hips open, shoulders square, weight left — and it stays left.
  • Small chest turn back, chest keeps rotating through — quiet wrists.
  • Runner: ball back of center, hands slightly ahead — or same stroke, less loft (8/9-iron).
First tee: hover the club · slow straight-back start · full turn · hands above the trail shoulder · quiet mind · finish facing the target, balanced.
